Before the Call

Your Job Before the Call

Once the confirmation team connects you with a lead, two things matter:

  • 1. Review the CRM notes.

Know their name, their business, what the confirmation team gathered. Walk in informed — not cold.

  • 2. Make contact and set the tone.

"Hey [Name], this is [Rep]. I'll be the one on your enrollment meeting on [day]. Really looking forward to it. I took a look at [their company] and I'm excited to dig into where you're headed. If there's anything you'd like me to know ahead of time, feel free to shoot it over."

COACHING NOTE:A lead who has had a real interaction with you before the call shows up at dramatically higher rates than one who only received automated messages.

STEP 2

The Warm-Up

First 60–90 seconds. Build rapport.

COACHING NOTE:Like → Listen → Believe → Buy. That's the progression. If they don't like you, they won't listen. If they don't listen, they won't believe. If they don't believe, they won't buy. The warm-up is where "like" happens.

2A. Open with warmth

"Hey [Name]! How's it going? Thanks for hopping on — I've been looking forward to this."

Then find something to connect on:

  • Where they're located — react to it, find common ground
  • Their business — "I was checking out [company] — how'd you get into that?"
  • Something visible on Zoom — office setup, background, anything real
COACHING NOTE:This is elicitation, not interrogation. You're making an observation that naturally causes them to volunteer information.

2B. Build commonality

Scan for things you share in common. Same state, same hobby, same type of business. Point it out when you find it. Commonality = trust shortcut.

2–3 minutes max. Don't let this stretch past 3 minutes.

2C. Identify the decision maker

"Are you the main decision maker on something like this, or is there a partner or spouse who'd want to be part of the conversation?"

COACHING NOTE:This is easy to miss and expensive to skip. If there's a spouse or business partner who needs to weigh in, you need to know NOW.

2D. Time check

"Before we dive in — do you have a hard stop today, or are we good on time?"

Key Principles — Step 2

PrincipleWhat It Means
Like → Listen → Believe → BuyThey have to like you first. Everything else follows.
Ask, don't tellLet them talk about themselves.
Elicitation > InterrogationMake observations that invite responses. Don't fire off questions.
Find common groundShared experiences = instant trust. Point it out when you find it.
Surface the decision maker earlyPartner or spouse involved? Find out now, not at the close.
STEP 3

Agenda Frame

Set expectations. Gain permission. Plant the decision seed.

COACHING NOTE:The brain is wired to want to know what's coming next. When someone doesn't know what to expect, their brain shifts into survival mode. The agenda frame removes the unknown so they can relax and engage.

3A. Introduce your role and frame the call

"Well [Name], thanks for taking the time to be here. I'm excited for this conversation. Let me give you a quick idea of how this works so we're on the same page.

I'm an enrollment specialist here at Scaling.com. I work closely with Dr. Hardy and Blake Erickson, and my role is really to take the time to understand you, your business, and where you're looking to go, and then figure out together if this is genuinely a good fit.

Because honestly, it's pretty black and white. Either you're looking to grow linearly, or you're looking to truly go for scale. We like to say that we're not a country club. We want to bring in the right entrepreneurs who are ready to go for 10x, and we take that seriously.

So what I'd love to do is start by diving into your journey up to this point, what's been working, what you'd want more of, and where the gaps are. From there, we usually get a pretty good understanding of whether this is a real fit. If it is, I'd be happy to walk you through what next steps look like. And if it's not, we'll take a few steps back and see what would need to align for this to be an effective partnership moving forward.

Does that sound okay?"

COACHING NOTE:This opening establishes authority, frames YOU as the evaluator, sets the "not a country club" tone, lays out the call structure, establishes indifference, and ends with your first micro-close.

3B. Cast the broad net

"Most business owners I talk with land here for one of three reasons. They've been doing really well but they've hit a ceiling and feel stuck. They're looking to step back or eventually exit but can't seem to get the business past themselves. Or they're seeing a lot of momentum, they have big goals, and they just haven't found the right pathway to get there yet. Which of those sounds most like you, or is it something completely different?"

COACHING NOTE:The prospect self-selects into a category (discovery starting point), it normalizes their situation, and even if none fit, they'll correct you with their real reason.

3C. Where they came from & what outcome they are looking for?

"I always like to ask: How'd you first hear about us?
- Mastermind?
- Fan of Dr. Hardy?
- Recent event?

Go two to three layers on this question. It's important for data reasons. For example, if they said they heard about the book "The Science of Scaling" from a mastermind, then ask which mastermind, where they're located, who started the mastermind, etc.

Key Principles — Step 3

PrincipleWhat It Means

"We're not a country club"Sets the tone. This is serious. Not everyone gets in.

STEP 4

Discovery — Identify the Gap

Current state → Desired state → What's in the way. This is where deals are won or lost.

COACHING NOTE:Discovery is the most important step in the entire sales process. If you nail discovery, closing is easy. If you rush it, you'll fight for every deal. Your job: understand (1) where they are now, (2) where they want to be, (3) what's in the way. The distance between 1 and 2 is the gap.

Two rules that never bend:

  • No solution giving. Acknowledge problems. Save solutions for the pitch.
  • No selfish discovery. Demonstrate expertise and create light-bulb moments IN the discovery.

15–25 minutes. Discovery should be the longest portion of your call.

4A. Probing — What brought them here?

"So you said you're feeling [their broad net answer]. Tell me more about that — what's going on in the business right now?"

"Was there a specific moment or event that made you say, 'Okay, I need to look into this'? A line-in-the-sand moment?"

4B. Situational — Where are they, and where are they going?

"Give me the lay of the land. What does the business look like right now — revenue, team size, how long you've been at it?"

"And where are you trying to take this? What's the goal?"

COACHING NOTE:Don't project goals onto them. Ask what THEIR goal is.

4C. Gap awareness — What's keeping them from getting there?

"So you're at [current state] and you want to get to [desired state]. What do you feel like is standing in the way?"

"If you had to point to the one thing that's holding the business back the most, what would it be?"

COACHING NOTE:Their words fall into two categories: Logistical/tactical ("We need more leads") and Emotional ("I feel stuck"). Write down both. Circle the emotional ones — that's where the deal gets made.

Digging Past Tactical to Emotional — Cheat Sheet

They say (tactical)What's underneath (emotional)How to dig
"We need to grow"Why? Scared? Proving something? Building toward an exit?"What's the big push for growth right now?"
"We need more revenue"Bleeding? Business at risk? Family depending on it?"How come it's not covering what you need?"
"I want to scale"Scale for what? Freedom? Legacy? To sell?"What does it look like on the other side of scaling?"
"We need better systems"Drowning? 80-hour weeks? Missing their kids' lives?"What's it costing you right now NOT having those systems?"

4D. Identify the buying driver — Pain or Goal?

If pain is dominant (stressed, frustrated, stuck):

"What kind of problems is this creating for you?"

"What happens if nothing changes in the next 12 months?"

If goal is dominant (ambitious, excited, forward-looking):

"What would hitting that goal actually mean for you — beyond the number?"

"How would your life change if you got there?"

4E. Solution awareness — What have they tried?

"Have you ever worked with a coach or a program before? What was that experience like?"

"What would make this time different for you?"

4F. Techniques that separate good from great

Repeat their last 5 words.

When you don't know where to go next, repeat the last thing they said and pause. They will elaborate every time.

The Rule of 3: Question → Label → Elicitation.

Ask a question. Label the emotion you hear. Then make a statement designed to get them to correct you.

4G. What NOT to do

MistakeWhy It Kills the Deal
Solution givingYou just removed their reason to stay on the call.
Selfish discovery20 minutes of questions where they learn nothing = resistance.
Stacking questionsNever ask two questions in a row. One at a time.
Making assumptionsIf you don't know, ASK.
Staying on the surface"We need more revenue" is a branch. The root is underneath.

Discovery Extras

Compliance → Commitment gradient

"Are you even open to working with someone on this?" (compliance — easy yes)

"Could you see yourself working hand-in-hand with a strategist?" (commitment — more specific)

"Are you more fact-finding right now, or have you reached a point where this is a real priority?" (trial close — forces a real answer)

Courtroom control — redirect language

"That's a great point — I want to come back to that. But first, let me ask you..."

"I appreciate you sharing that. Let's bookmark it — I think it'll make more sense once I show you how we work."

PrincipleWhat It Means

Pain or Goal — know the driverThe dominant buying driver changes your entire approach.
STEP 5

Information Confirmation

Prove you listened. Expand the gap. Bridge to the pitch.

COACHING NOTE:This is the bridge between discovery and pitching. If done properly, the sale is roughly 70% made before you ever show the offer.

5A. Recap what you heard

"So let me make sure I've got this right. You're at [revenue/stage], you've been at this for [X years], and right now the big thing is [their core challenge]. You want to get to [their goal], and the main thing standing in the way is [their gap]. Does that capture it, or is there anything I'm missing?"

5B. Add "hot sauce" — expand the gap

Don't just repeat what they said. Reflect it back with the full emotional weight.

What they said: "I can't generate leads and it's been a huge headache."

"You said something that stuck with me — that one of the things keeping you up at night has been the fact that you can't generate leads consistently, and without that, the revenue can't grow the way you need it to."

COACHING NOTE:Once they confirm this version, it becomes an obligatory statement — their words, in their voice.

5C. Soft trial close

"Based on everything we've talked about — if I could show you a program that specifically addresses [their gap] and helps you get to [their goal], is that something you'd want to see?"

5D. Bridge to pitch

"You've named some things that really stand out. I'm going to go over what we do and specifically how we work with business owners on exactly these kinds of challenges — and honestly, some of what you've shared is right in our wheelhouse. Let me walk you through it."

Key Principles — Step 5

PrincipleWhat It Means
Sum it up simplyRecap their situation, goal, and gap. Show you were paying attention.
Their words become their truthOnce they confirm the amplified version, it's locked in.
STEP 6

Pitch Personalization — Story, Pitch, Pillars

Make them feel like this was built for them.

COACHING NOTE:Three parts, every time: Story → Pitch → Pillars. Personalize everything to what they told you in discovery. Eliminate I, Me, Mine — third-party credibility always.

6A. Story — The Scaling.com existence narrative

"Let me give you some quick background on how this all came together.

Dr. Ben Hardy spent years researching what separates businesses that scale from the ones that stay stuck. Real research, real data, published work. Blake Erickson was on the other side — a real operator who built, scaled, and sold businesses.

They came together because they realized most business owners aren't missing effort or ambition. They're missing the framework, the right strategy, and someone in their corner who's already been where they're trying to go.

That's why Scaling.com exists — Frame, Floor, Focus. Sound familiar?"

6B. Power Pillars

Personalize each pillar to their discovery. After each one, get them talking.

PILLAR 1: One-on-One Scaling Strategist

  • Dedicated strategist who knows their business, numbers, and goals
  • 24/7 access — text, email, WhatsApp
  • Bi-weekly 1 hour strategy sessions + unlimited supplemental calls

PILLAR 2: The Community

  • Over 500 members — $1M to over a billion in revenue
  • Direct access to Dr. Hardy and Blake on live group coaching
  • A network of operators who've solved the problems they're working through

PILLAR 3: The Scaling Portal

  • Scaler AI trained on Hardy's frameworks and thousands of hours of conversations
  • Full content library, community feed, member directory, progress dashboard
  • All coaching recordings saved — nothing gets lost

PILLAR 4: Live Events

  • In-person events at world-class venues
  • World-class speakers
  • Relationships built at events often worth more than anything else

6C. The Tie-Back & Investment Anchor

"So that's the full picture — strategist with 24/7 access, the community, the Scaling Portal and AI, and live events.

The investment is [Price] for the year paid in full — that's what most of our members do because it saves you [X]. But there's also the option of [Price] a month for 12 months.

What parts of all this do you feel like are going to be the highest leverage for where you're at right now?"

COACHING NOTE:End by getting them to sell it back to you. When they tell you which parts matter most, their brain is programming itself for the decision.

Key Principles — Step 6

PrincipleWhat It Means
Personalize to discoveryTheir words, their problems, their goals — woven into every pillar.
Eliminate I, Me, MineThird-party credibility. Reference clients at the next tier up.
Don't sell features — sell accessWhat does it DO for them? Not what does it INCLUDE.
STEP 7

Trial Close & Investment

Take their temperature. Stack commitments. Drop the number. Let the silence work.

COACHING NOTE:If you've executed Steps 1-6 well, the close should be the easiest part of the call.

7A. Temperature check — scale of 1 to 10

"Before we go any further, I just want to get a quick read on where you're at. On a scale of 1 to 10 — 1 being 'this was a total waste of my time' and 10 being 'this is exactly what I've been looking for' — where would you put yourself?"

7B. Justification — make them sell it back to you

If they're a 7, 8, 9, or 10:

"That's great to hear. What specifically is resonating with you that you'd rate yourself that high?"

If they're below a 7:

"Okay — what would have to change for you to say 10?"

COACHING NOTE:When they tell you what resonated, their subconscious hears them say it. If THEY say it, it's gospel.

7C. Isolate to the investment

"So other than the actual investment itself — is there anything else that would stop you from getting started?"

7D. Commitment question — lock it in

"So what I'm hearing is — if the investment makes sense for where you're at, you're ready to get started. Is that right?"

COACHING NOTE:Compliance was "yeah, this sounds good." Agreement was "I can see how this would help." Commitment is "yes, if the money works, I'm in." That's a fundamentally different answer.

7E. The investment conversation

"Like I mentioned — the investment is [Price] for the year paid in full. That's what most of our members do because it saves [X]. There's also the option of [Price] a month for 12 months."

Then stop talking.

7F. Reading the response

If they're ready: Move directly to Step 8. Don't sell past the close.

If they have questions: Answer directly, then re-close:

"Does that answer your question? Any other concerns before we move forward?"

7G. The doubt/reversal technique

"Hey — I could be totally off here, but it seems like maybe something shifted. Are we still on the same page, or is there something I'm missing?"

7H. "The truth is easy"

COACHING NOTE:Real answers come fast. When answers get hard, something's hidden.

"When you say you need to think about it — is there something specific, or is it more of a general feeling?"

"If you woke up tomorrow and knew 100% this was the right move, what would still need to happen between now and then?"

7I. Walk away after 4-5 loops

"Look — I can tell this is something you're taking seriously, and I respect that. I don't want to push you into something that doesn't feel right. Here's what I'd suggest..."

Key Principles — Step 7

PrincipleWhat It Means
Compliance ≠ Commitment"Sounds good" is compliance. "I'm ready to start" is commitment.
Make them sell it to themselvesWhen they justify their rating, their brain programs itself for the decision.
Isolate before you closeNarrow it to one variable before asking for commitment.
Silence is a closer's friendDrop the number, stop talking. First to speak has the pressure.
The truth is easyReal answers come fast. Hesitation means something's hidden.
If you feel it, say itName it when something feels off.
Don't sell past the closeIf they're ready, stop talking and start enrolling.
Walk away with dignityAfter 3-4 loops, respect the no.
STEP 8

Close & Enrollment

Walk them through the membership agreement & payment. Lock in the follow-up if needed.

TRACK A: They're Ready — Enroll

8A. Assume the close

"Awesome — let's get you set up. Next steps are super simple. We're going to get you on the Scaling Portal — everything I just showed you, all the incredible features there. But the most important thing: we're going to assign you your strategist in the next 24 hours and schedule that first strategy call within the next 7 days. Sound good?"

COACHING NOTE:Lead with what they GET, not what they SIGN. The agreement is just process.

8B. Agreement walkthrough — on the call

"Cool — I'm going to send over the membership agreement to your email right now. It'll take about 60 seconds to come through."

COACHING NOTE:Do NOT just send the agreement and hope they sign it later. The agreement happens ON the call.

8C. Get them set up — stay on the call

"I'll be right here on the call to help you through everything and get you enrolled and onboarded so that you have access to everything. I just want to make sure you get set up in the Scaling Portal and you get the link to schedule your first strategy call in the next week or so."

"Go ahead and pull that up — it should be in your inbox now. I'll be right here if you have any questions as you go through it."

PRO TIP:While they're reading the agreement, casually say you're going to grab a quick sip of water or top off your glass. Takes the awkwardness out of the silence.

8D. Ask for a referral

"This is going to be a great partnership. One more thing — do you know anyone else in your world who's in a similar spot? Someone who's scaling a business and could benefit from the same kind of support? We get a lot of our best members through referrals from people like you."

TRACK B: They're Not Ready — Follow-Up Locking System

8E. Qualify the follow-up

COACHING NOTE:A follow-up is only valid with a genuine, specific logistical reason they cannot move forward today. "I need to think about it" is not a reason — it's an avoidance mechanism.

Before you set a follow-up, you must have clear answers to:

  • 1. What is their buying motive? (from discovery — should be crystal clear)
  • 2. What is the specific reason they can't move forward today?
  • 3. Is this reason resolvable, and when?

"I totally respect that. When are you going to connect with [partner]?"

"If [partner] gives the green light, are we good to go?"

8F. Between-call touch

Day before the follow-up call:

"Hey [Name] — we've got our call tomorrow at [time]. I know you were going to [their specific action]. How did that go?"

COACHING NOTE:Reference the specific thing they said they'd do. If you nailed down specifics on the call, you have something real to ask about. If not, you're stuck sending "just checking in" — and that's when you get ghosted.

8G. ICC Objection Framework — Isolate, Clarify, Counsel

I — Isolate: What exactly is the concern?

"Help me understand — when you say [their objection], what specifically do you mean by that?"

"Is that the only thing holding you back, or is there something else too?"

C — Clarify: Get the full picture before you respond.

"So if I'm hearing you right, the concern is [restate it]. Is that accurate?"

"And if we could address that — would everything else be a green light?"

C — Counsel: Now — and only now — address it.

"I appreciate you being upfront about that. Here's what I'd say..."

COACHING NOTE:Most reps jump straight to Counsel. Don't solve the objection until you fully understand it.

8H. Common objections — specific language

"I need to think about it."

"I respect that. Can I ask — what specifically are you wanting to think through? Is it the investment, whether this is the right fit, or something else?"

"I need to talk to my partner/spouse."

"Totally get that. Help me understand: does your partner know you're exploring this? How do they feel about you investing in the business this way?"

"If your partner gives the green light — are we all in?"

"It's too expensive."

"I hear you. Let me ask — is it that the investment doesn't make sense for the value, or is it more of a cash flow timing thing?"

"I've been burned by coaching before."

"I'm sorry to hear that. What happened? What was missing?"

"What would make this time different for you?"

"The timing isn't right."

"I understand. Help me out — when you say the timing isn't right, is there something specific that needs to happen first?"

8I. Walk away after 4-5 loops

"I respect where you're at. I've given you everything I can — and I think you know whether this is the right move for you. I'm not going to push you into something. If you decide this is what you want, I'm here. Just reach out."
